Reflection

A short review of the Problem 9 run, using the ticket Actual sections and Cash tab as evidence.

1. Agent performance

Ticket 101: Strong performance. Inventory correctly found zero size-S tees and a one-unit shortage; Accounting caught that the open $840 rush-reprint invoice needed reconciliation; Customer Service drafted a careful message without promising a date. The Boss made the right hold decision.

Ticket 102: Partially complete but safe. Facilities correctly identified the $2,400 lease obligation and overdue timing. The Boss escalated it, but Accounting did not run as a separate agent, so payment status and late fees remained unresolved. No unsupported payment was made.

Ticket 103: Good analytical performance. Inventory found 8 size-M hoodies and a shortage of 12, while Accounting calculated the $22 cost and $58 list price. The team correctly refused to invent a discount or vendor availability. The missing vendor quote prevented a final commercial decision.

2. Actual versus Expected

101: The Actual matched the Expected routing: Boss → Inventory, then Accounting and Customer Service. It used the planned inventory, vendor, pricing, cash, and invoice tools.

102: The Actual was narrower than Expected: Boss → Facilities happened, but the planned follow-up to Accounting did not run. Facilities still identified the correct next step, which was safer than guessing or paying.

103: The Actual matched the Expected specialist path: Boss → Inventory → Accounting. Customer Service was not needed because no approved customer-facing offer existed. The Cash tab confirms all three tickets changed cash by $0.00 because no human approval occurred.

3. What could be simpler as one agent with tools?

Ticket 102 could be handled more simply by one Facilities-oriented agent with get_ticket_lease and get_shop_cash. It only needed to identify the $2,400 rent obligation and flag Accounting approval; the multi-agent handoff added little value because no payment was authorized. A single agent could still be required to stop before changing cash.

4. Three new problems this team could solve

  1. A customer asks whether a specific hoodie size is available: Inventory can use get_ticket_inventory, and Customer Service can draft a factual reply.
  2. Staff receive a vendor invoice and need to know whether it is overdue and affordable: Accounting can combine get_ticket_invoice, get_shop_cash, and the desk date, then request human approval.
  3. The landlord sends another rent reminder: Facilities can use get_ticket_lease, compare the due date, and route a verified payment recommendation to Accounting.

5. Three problems this team cannot solve yet

  1. It cannot actually source the 12 missing size-M hoodies from QuickShip CT. The current vendor tool has lead time and unpaid-invoice status, but no vendor product catalog, availability, quote, or shipping cost. Inventory would need a vendor-catalog/quote tool and a procurement workflow.
  2. It cannot resolve a broken refrigerator or other store repair. There is no maintenance table or facilities work-order tool. Facilities would need repair-ticket creation, asset history, technician availability, and cost-estimate tools.
  3. It cannot confirm whether rent or an invoice was already paid, issue a refund, or send a customer message. The team can prepare a payment and a draft, but it lacks payment reconciliation/refund tools and an approved outbound messaging service. Accounting and Customer Service would need those tools plus stronger approval and delivery auditing.
